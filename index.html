Section 1 • Introduction & Scope

Understanding ODL & Programme Thresholds

ODL refers to the provision of flexible educational opportunities in terms of access and multiple modes of knowledge acquisition, anchored by student engagement.

1. Flexible

The availability of choices for educational endeavours anywhere, anytime and anyhow, supporting continual skills upgrading and reskilling.

2. Access

Opportunity made available to all, freeing learners from constraints of time and place and fulfilling fundamental rights to learning.

3. Multiple Modes

Use of various delivery systems and learning resources—self-instructional materials (SIM), synchronous & asynchronous e-learning platforms.

Sections 3, 4 & 5 • Process & Governance

Accreditation Lifecycle & External Evaluation

Programme accreditation progresses through three distinct stages of evaluation—Provisional, Full, and Compliance—supported by Internal Self-Review and an independent Panel of Assessors (POA).

Stage 1 • Pre-Launch MQA-01-ODL

Provisional Accreditation

Determines whether a proposed ODL programme meets the minimum quality standards prior to its launch. Emphasises curriculum design and preparatory arrangements.

  • Requires Part A (HEP Profile), Part B (Programme Description), and Part C (Programme Standards).
  • Must include Self-Instructional Materials (SIM) of Semester 1 (Section 6, 3.1.2.5b).
Outcome Registry: List of Provisionally Accredited Programmes
Stage 2 • Operational MQA-02-ODL

Full Accreditation

Ascertains that teaching, learning, support systems, and all related activities of a provisionally accredited programme meet the quality standards in practice.

  • Requires updated Part A, B, C plus Part D: Programme Self-Review Report (PSRR).
  • Must provide SIM for ALL courses in the programme (Section 6, 3.1.2.5b).
Outcome Registry: Malaysian Qualifications Register (MQR)
Stage 3 • Every 5 Years MQA-04

Compliance Evaluation

Monitors and ensures the maintenance and enhancement of fully accredited programmes. Carried out at least once in five years.

  • HEP conducts periodic Programme Self-Review (PSR) and submits MQA-04 with endorsed evidence.
  • Failure to maintain quality may lead to revocation and a cessation date recorded in MQR.
Outcome Registry: Continued MQR Registration
Section 4.3.1 & Section 5

The 5 Key Parties in Programme Accreditation

1. MQA Officer Coordinator & Secretariat

Assigned for every application; coordinates with the HEP, accompanies POA during the evaluation visit, and provides administrative & reporting support.

2. HEP Liaison Officer HEP Resource Person

Appointed by the HEP to coordinate with the MQA officer, arrange the tentative schedule for the evaluation visit, and inform all relevant stakeholders of the audit plan.

3. Representatives of the HEP Management, Staff & Students

Provide evidence and walkthroughs of facilities/LMS. Academic staff are interviewed regarding staff development, workload distribution, PEO/PLO understanding, and academic culture.

4. Chairperson of the Panel of Assessors (POA) Team Facilitator & Lead Author

Experienced assessor who fosters critical professional discussion, presents the Oral Exit Report, and collates member inputs into an evidence-based, coherent Final Evaluation Report.

5. Members of the Panel of Assessors (POA) Subject & ODL QA Experts

Selected for subject expertise, curriculum design, and QA skills. Assess compliance with MQF and the 7 areas, verify PSRR & improvement plans, and reach objective, rational judgment free of conflict of interest.

Section 4.1 • Programme Self-Review (PSR)

5 Guiding Questions for Internal Quality Audit

When addressing the seven areas of evaluation in a Programme Self-Review Report (PSRR), departments must consider:

  1. i. What actions are undertaken in relation to these quality areas? Why were these actions chosen? Are they appropriate?
  2. ii. How do we check their effectiveness? What performance indicators do we have, and are they appropriate?
  3. iii. What do we do as a result of the review?
  4. iv. Can we measure the degree of achievements? What are the actual outcomes?
  5. v. Can we improve on the existing actions, even on those that are already effective?
Section 4.6 • Appeals Under MQA Act 679

Right to Appeal Accreditation Decisions

An HEP can appeal against the decision of the MQA Accreditation Committee or professional bodies based on factual contents, substantive errors, or substantive inconsistencies between the oral exit report, final evaluation report, and committee decision.

Non-Professional Programmes

Submitted to Department of Higher Education, Ministry of Education (MOE) for consideration by the Minister of Education.

Professional Programmes

Submitted to the professional body through MQA for consideration by the Appellate Body set up by the respective body.

Page ii • Official Foreword

Foreword to COPPA: ODL (Second Edition)

With the advent of technology, higher education institutions are no longer confined to the traditional brick and mortar learning and teaching methods. Open and Distance Learning (ODL) is gaining its momentum and is the new frontier of learning which will revolutionise the landscape of higher education. ODL presents a potpourri of benefits where it transforms education to be more flexible and accessible to meet the needs of the 21st century learners.

As the number of institutions that will be launching the ODL programmes is expected to increase further in the future, quality assurance of ODL programmes is crucial. Hence to safeguard the integrity and credibility of ODL, the Code of Practice for Programme Accreditation: ODL (COPPA: ODL) has been developed specifically to meet the increasing demand of ODL programmes offered by full-fledged open universities, traditional and dual mode institutions in Malaysia.

The COPPA: ODL contains clear, specific indicators and benchmark standards that will guide the institutions in the development, delivery, assessment as well as the monitoring and review of the ODL programme.
